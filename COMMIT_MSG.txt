elarxml batch 4: filename scheme, batching policy, PULL manifest

The naming and the batching, which together decide how many files come out and
what they are called.

BatchNaming reproduces the legacy scheme exactly, and two things about it are
worth stating because neither is visible from a filename. The C152100 segment
is a synthetic clock rather than a timestamp or a sequence: it starts at
output.start_time, or at the run's wall-clock time when that is not set, and
advances by exactly sixty seconds per batch. Two runs of the same feed on the
same day with the same explicit start_time therefore collide; with it unset -
which is how the live configuration ships - they produce different names, so
nothing is overwritten but duplicate deliverables accumulate with nothing to
say so. That is the real risk, and countSameDayPairs reports how many pairs
for the current day are already in the output directory rather than refusing
the run, because refusing would break the case where a re-run is most needed,
immediately after a partial failure.

The D26229 segment is built arithmetically, from getDayOfYear and a format
string. That is deliberate: in java.time DD is the day of the year, which is
exactly the trap that took the validate executor down in production, and
building the segment with arithmetic means the pattern is never written at
all. ofPattern now appears nowhere in the package and the rule scan asserts
it, while the test keeps a control assertion recording that ofPattern("DD") on
17 August does indeed yield 229 - so the reason for the arithmetic stays
visible to whoever reads it next.

BatchPolicy implements the one-rule decision from the spec. Its describe
method names the rule in force and, under BYTES, names the ignored
max_index_docs with its value, because that limit sits in the family's
properties file where anyone can read it and would otherwise silently stop
mattering. A rule constructed without its own limit is refused at once rather
than batching in some unintended way. An oversize document closes the open
batch before being written alone, since it is not alone if the open batch is
left around it; under FAIL the message explains that the document can never be
written and gives both ways out. estimateDrifted flags an estimator that has
come loose, because a budget built on a wrong estimate would roll over in the
wrong place and nothing downstream would ever say so.

PullTemplate writes the manifest with [INDEX_NAME] substituted in every
attribute of every element. That is a superset of the legacy behaviour, which
substituted only inside Index elements in a hardcoded namespace, and it cannot
change any file the legacy tool produced since a template carrying the
placeholder anywhere else was already broken. The legacy inconsistency where
the PULL generator ignores idms.namespace and hardcodes a different one from
the INDX template is preserved rather than tidied: the receiving system has
been accepting it, and a rewrite is not the place to find out whether it would
accept anything else.

Verified with 61 assertions compiled with --release 8 and run. The reported
filename is reproduced exactly and its PULL shares the counter by
construction; the clock advances sixty seconds per batch and an hour after
sixty of them; the Julian segment is correct at day 1, day 229, day 366 of a
leap year and year 2000; the day rolls only after files_per_julian_date pairs
and the clock wraps past midnight rather than overflowing; start_time
validation names the property for a value that is too short, an impossible
hour and letters; same-day pairs are counted without blocking a re-run; both
batching rules behave including the boundary where a document lands exactly on
the limit; the oversize document closes the open batch first and FAIL explains
itself; a rule with no limit is refused at construction; and the PULL is
substituted everywhere with no placeholder surviving and its fixed content
untouched. The batch 1, 2 and 3 suites were re-run and pass.

One assertion of mine was wrong rather than the code: an expression for "one
second before midnight" that did not evaluate to it. Corrected, and split into
two clearer cases.

NOT verified: mvn clean package, and the naming against a real output
directory. The filename is the cheapest thing to check on deploy - run the
step once and the first INDX should be indistinguishable in name from one the
legacy tool would have produced at the same start_time.
