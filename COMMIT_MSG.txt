elarxml: the drift check was blind, and staging reused one filename

Two defects from the first run on volume, and a correction to what was said
about the first of them.

THE DRIFT CHECK COMPARED TWO QUANTITIES THAT WERE NEVER COMPARABLE

The step log filled with "the byte budget is rolling at the wrong point", on
every single document, at a fixed ratio of 1.330 between the two figures. That
is 4/3, the Base64 expansion, and it is the whole explanation.

Three quantities are involved and only two of them are the same kind of thing:
the estimate, which is encoded characters; what writeDocument returned, which
is the raw payload read from the document; and what the INDX actually grew by,
which nothing measured. The check compared the first against the second, so it
was always 4/3 apart and always fired. A warning that always fires reports
nothing, and this is the one that exists to police PER_DOCUMENT_OVERHEAD, the
only number in this executor that is not derived from something else. It had
been blind since it was written.

A correction, on the record. It was first reported to the field that the byte
budget itself counted raw bytes, which would have made a 1.9 GB budget produce
INDX files of about 2.5 GB. That was wrong. policy.appended(estimate)
accumulates the encoded estimate, so the budget was in the right units and the
delivered files were the right size. The conclusion was drawn from the warning
rather than from the line that accumulates, and reading that line first would
have cost nothing.

The batch now measures its own growth, charsWritten before and after the
document, and that figure feeds both the budget and the drift check. Measured
rather than derived, so it needs no assumption about encoding overhead and
stays correct if the formatting changes. The counter keeps the raw payload,
which is what bytesEmbedded means.

THE STAGING FILE REUSED ONE NAME TENS OF THOUSANDS OF TIMES

The same run failed after 27668 documents and 9.3 GB with
"elarxml-content.part (Access is denied)", on a disk with plenty of free
space, confirmed.

Every document was staged to the same path, deleted and recreated. On Windows
that is precisely the shape that collides with a virus scanner or the indexer
still holding the previous file. It is not a permissions problem, it is a
race, which is why it took tens of thousands of iterations to appear.

Each staged document now gets its own name, so it cannot collide with the one
before it. Creation retries three times with a short back-off, because such a
lock lasts fractions of a second. And because unique names never overwrite one
another, the staging directory is swept once per run for anything a killed run
left behind. The failure message now says the name was unique, so nobody is
sent looking for a leftover that cannot exist.

VERIFIED
- The drift check, on the document sizes taken from the real log, 236 KB to
  2.13 MB: 5 warnings out of 5 before the change, 0 out of 5 after, with the
  delivered INDX byte-identical. What is written did not change; only what is
  compared.
- 167 assertions on the IFS store, up from 162: consecutive documents get
  different staging paths, a planted leftover is swept, one staged file exists
  at a time and none after close.
- The end-to-end identity still holds, both lookup strategies still deliver
  the same file, and all seven elar suites pass unchanged. Rule scans clean.

NOT VERIFIED
- The Windows lock itself. It needs Windows and a scanner, neither of which
  exists in the chat sandbox, so the staging change removes the mechanism
  rather than demonstrating the cure. The next run on volume is the evidence.
- mvn clean package: Maven Central is unreachable from the sandbox, so the
  build on the target machine remains the only final proof.
- The report that an elarcheck step will not expand in the designer could not
  be reproduced. A four-step workflow with elarxml in IFS renders the last
  card with its body present, 26 fields inside it, div-balanced and inside its
  own card; the CSS rule and toggleNode are both correct. Three hypotheses
  were ruled out by measurement and none remains. The panel suite was weak by
  construction, asserting on innerHTML as a string, which passes on malformed
  HTML, and now counts elements inside the card instead.
