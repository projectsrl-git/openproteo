Linked entities in the JSON viewer (batch 1), with three field fixes

This supersedes the earlier json-linked-entities patch, which had not been
pushed: it carries batch 1 of
.claude/2026-08-11-DESIGN-linked-entity-viewers.md AND the direction check
below. Apply this one only.

Batch 1 gives the standalone JSON page several files at once with a memory
budget counted in MODEL RECORDS rather than megabytes - measured, not guessed:
a 2.1 MB document gave 168 329 records and 37 MB of heap, about eighteen times
the file, so a megabyte limit would have been wrong by an order of magnitude.
A file that would not fit is refused without ever being materialised and the
files already open are untouched. It also gives declared relationships between
entities, links clickable from both ends, and the focus/parent/child diagram
in the shape of EOR_viewer.html, walked one hop at a time because five
thousand entities cannot be drawn at once. The relationship question is asked
per file AFTER parsing rather than at page open as the request put it, since
before a file is parsed there are no fields for the dropdowns; that was
flagged in the spec and agreed.

Then the direction check, which is what the screenshots were about.

Reported with screenshots: the diagram showed a RELATIONSHIP as the focused
entity and a CUSTOMER as its child, which reads backwards. The code was doing
exactly what it had been told - the declaration was child
$.customers.customerId pointing at parent
$.customers[].relationships.relatedCustomerId, the two sides the wrong way
round. The defect is in the interface, which accepted that without a word and
even reassured the operator that it had resolved.

A resolution rate on its own cannot detect this, which is the whole problem:
in the inverted direction 3 315 of 5 000 values resolved, so the number looked
perfectly healthy and the mistake only surfaced later as a diagram that read
the wrong way. Two signals do detect it, and both must agree before the page
says anything. A PARENT field is an identity, so it should be unique, while a
CHILD field is a reference and usually is not; and the correct direction
resolves a higher share than the reverse. wsLinkStats now computes, per side,
how many distinct values a field has, out of how many rows, and whether it is
unique, together with the resolution rate in each direction.

When both signals agree the banner says these sides look swapped, explains in
one sentence why a parent field should be unique and a child field need not
be, and offers a button that turns the relationship round in place and
re-reports it. The same thing is available before the mistake is made: a Swap
sides button exchanges the six dropdowns, and the panel now spells out the
customers example rather than describing the rule abstractly.

Requiring both signals matters. A legitimately many-to-one key - orders
pointing at customers, where the parent is unique and the child is not - is
the normal case and must not be warned about, and neither is a case where the
parent key genuinely repeats. Only the combination of a non-unique parent, a
unique child and a better reverse rate is suspicious.

Verified with jsdom against a document reproducing the reported shape - 300
unique customers and about 400 relationship rows whose relatedCustomerId
repeats, which is precisely why the bare rate could not tell. Twenty-four
assertions: the backwards declaration is flagged as an error rather than
accepted, the banner names the non-unique parent field and reports both
directions, the offered button turns it round, after which the parent side is
unique and the warning is gone and the listed link reads relationships to
customers; the diagram then focuses a customer with relationships as its
neighbours instead of the other way round; declaring it correctly the first
time raises nothing; the Swap sides button exchanges all four path and field
selects before adding; and a legitimate many-to-one link passes silently. The
batch-1 suite was re-run as a regression and passes, with one assertion
updated to the new wording.

Three further things were reported from the field on top of that and are in
here too.

The expand and collapse control was a ten-pixel triangle. It is now a plus or
a minus in a bordered box: it is the only way to open a node, and a small
target is a bad target.

The diagram put a neighbour on the side its DECLARATION implied rather than
the side its DIRECTION implies. The declaration calls the field that holds the
value the CHILD field, which is the database convention, but on a picture the
record doing the pointing reads as the one above: a relationship row naming a
customer is that customer's parent. So the left column is now everything that
points AT the focus and the right column is what the focus points at, and the
neighbour counter says so in the same terms. Whether an entity is drawn left
or right no longer depends on which end of the declaration it happens to sit
on.

And a badge now names the row it lives inside. A customer whose own
relationships list was empty showed four relationship badges and looked as
though the diagram had invented them; they were real, and they belonged to
four other customers. One line of context - "in UKC0003584" - turns a correct
answer that looks wrong into a correct answer that looks right, which was the
actual complaint.

Verified additionally against the reported shape exactly, a customer with an
empty relationships list named by four rows living inside three other
customers, with 26 assertions: only plus and minus appear as toggles and no
triangle survives; the four neighbours are labelled PARENT, drawn on the left
by measured x geometry, and each names the row it lives in with the two rows
of the same owner both attributed to it; focusing a relationship instead puts
its customer on the right as a CHILD; and the counter matches the sides drawn.
The two earlier suites were re-run, one stale assertion updated to the new
side convention.
